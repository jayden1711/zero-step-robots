This looks like a planar one-legged hopper: obs[0] is height, obs[1] is torso pitch, obs[2:5] are the three joint angles, and obs[8:11] are the joint velocities. I can't test anything, so I went for reliable survival rather than forward speed. Staying healthy for all 1000 steps earns about 1000 reward, and an attempt at a gait could fall early and earn much less.

**How it works:**
- Three hidden units act as saturating PD controllers, one per joint. Each holds its joint near 0, which is the straight-leg standing pose: `u = -tanh(Kp·q + Kd·q̇)`. The tanh limits the torque smoothly.
- The hip unit also gets mild feedback from torso pitch, so the hip pushes the torso back toward upright.
- The derivative gains are small so the loop stays stable with only 0.008 s between control updates.
- The other 13 hidden units are unused (all weights zero).

The idea is that a stiff, straight body standing on its foot is statically stable when its centre of mass is above the foot. I haven't verified this in simulation, so the score is unconfirmed.
